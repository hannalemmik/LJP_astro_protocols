# Preparation of chemically competent *E. coli* cells (CaCl₂ method)

**Author:** Hanna Lemmik  
**Source:** Lab notebook, pp. 14–16 (22–24 Sept 2026)

## Abstract

This protocol describes how to make chemically competent *E. coli* cells using calcium chloride. Cells are grown to early/mid-log phase (OD600 0.4–0.5), washed in ice-cold 0.1 M CaCl₂, resuspended in 0.1 M CaCl₂ with 15% glycerol and frozen in 100 µl aliquots at −80 °C.

## Guidelines

- Everything that must stay sterile (solutions, tubes, tips) should only be opened under the circulating-air cabinet. Sterility is lost otherwise.
- Keep cells and solutions cold at all times once the culture is harvested.
- Resuspend pellets gently; do not vortex, and avoid drawing cells up into the pipette filter.
- Prepare the reagents the day before and the preculture the evening before.

## Materials

- *E. coli* strain to be made competent (glycerol stock at −80 °C)
- Sterile LB broth
- Calcium chloride: anhydrous CaCl₂ (110.98 g/mol) or CaCl₂·2H₂O (147.01 g/mol)
- Glycerol, 100%
- Sterile (autoclaved) water
- 0.22 µm filter units
- 14 ml round-bottom culture tube with dual-position snap cap, sterile
- Sterile disposable 10 µl inoculation loops (blue)
- 2 × 50 ml Falcon tubes, sterile
- Sterile 1.5 ml microcentrifuge tubes (about 40)
- Refrigerated centrifuge (4 °C) with rotor for 50 ml tubes
- Shaking incubator at 37 °C
- Spectrophotometer (Eppendorf BioPhotometer) and disposable cuvettes
- Ice bucket, dry ice, −80 °C freezer

## Before start

Autoclave a few litres of water. Chill the 0.1 M CaCl₂ and 0.1 M CaCl₂ + 15% glycerol solutions at 4 °C before use.

## Procedure

### A. Reagent preparation

| Solution | Recipe | Use |
|---|---|---|
| 1 M CaCl₂ (500 ml) | 55.5 g anhydrous CaCl₂ **or** 73.5 g CaCl₂·2H₂O, water to 500 ml | Stock |
| 0.1 M CaCl₂ (200 ml) | 20 ml 1 M CaCl₂ + 180 ml sterile water | Washing |
| 0.1 M CaCl₂ + 15% glycerol (200 ml) | 20 ml 1 M CaCl₂ + 30 ml (≈ 37.8 g) glycerol, water to 200 ml | Final resuspension |

1. Prepare the 1 M CaCl₂ stock.
   *Check which form of CaCl₂ you have: 55 g of the dihydrate in 500 ml gives only ~0.75 M.*
2. Filter-sterilise with a 0.22 µm filter under the cabinet. Do not autoclave. Store cold.
   *Filtering alone is preferred: autoclaving can cause CaCO₃ or CaSO₄ precipitates with sulfate-containing water.*
3. Prepare the 0.1 M CaCl₂ washing solution. Filter-sterilise under the cabinet and store at 4 °C.
4. Prepare the 0.1 M CaCl₂ + 15% glycerol solution. Filter-sterilise under the cabinet and store at 4 °C.
   *Glycerol is viscous; measuring it by weight is more accurate: 30 ml ≈ 37.8 g.*
5. Under the cabinet, label about 40 sterile 1.5 ml tubes for the aliquots.

### B. Preculture (evening before)

1. Clean the cabinet with 70% ethanol and turn on the airflow. Open sterile LB, tubes and loops only inside the cabinet, keep lids off as briefly as possible, and never touch the open end of a tube or the tip of a loop.
2. Pipette 1 ml sterile LB into a sterile 14 ml round-bottom culture tube with a dual-position snap cap.
   *No antibiotic unless the strain carries a resistance marker.*
3. Take the glycerol stock from −80 °C and keep it on dry ice (or in a pre-chilled cryo block). Do not let it thaw.
   *Freeze–thaw cycles reduce the viability of the stock.*
4. With a sterile disposable 10 µl (blue) inoculation loop, scrape a little frozen material from the surface of the stock. Close the stock immediately and return it to −80 °C.
5. Swirl the loop in the LB, then discard the loop.
6. Close the snap cap on the first click (loose/vented position) so air can get in.
7. Label (strain, date, initials) and grow overnight (~16 h) at 37 °C with shaking (~200–250 rpm).
   *A turbid culture the next morning means it grew. If it is clear, repeat with a slightly larger scrape.*

### C. Growth to mid-log phase

1. One hour before starting, pre-warm 79.2 ml LB broth at 37 °C.
2. Add 800 µl of the overnight preculture (1:100 dilution).
3. Shake at 37 °C until OD600 reaches 0.4–0.5 (about 2 h 30 min–3 h). Measure OD600 as described in section D.
4. While the culture grows, place 2 × 50 ml Falcon tubes at 4 °C, put the CaCl₂ solutions on ice, and turn on the centrifuge at 4 °C.

### D. Measuring OD600 (Eppendorf BioPhotometer)

1. Turn on the BioPhotometer and let it run its self-check.
2. Select the OD600 method.
3. Fill a disposable cuvette with 1 ml sterile LB, place it with the clear sides facing the light path, and press **Blank**.
   *There is usually an arrow on the cuvette holder showing the light path.*
4. Swap in a cuvette with about 1 ml of well-mixed culture and press **Sample**.
5. Repeat every 15 min until the OD600 reaches 0.4.
   *No need to re-blank each time unless the machine was turned off or the medium changed.*

### E. Harvesting and CaCl₂ treatment

1. Divide the culture into 2 × 40 ml in the pre-chilled Falcon tubes.
2. Centrifuge for 10 min at 4000 rpm, 4 °C.
3. Discard the supernatant and gently resuspend each pellet in 20 ml ice-cold 0.1 M CaCl₂.
   *Avoid drawing the cells up into the pipette filter.*
4. Incubate for 20 min at 4 °C (on ice).
5. During the incubation, pre-chill the labelled sterile 1.5 ml tubes at −80 °C.
6. Centrifuge for 10 min at 4000 rpm, 4 °C.
7. Discard the supernatant and gently resuspend each pellet in 2 ml ice-cold 0.1 M CaCl₂ + 15% glycerol.
8. Aliquot 100 µl into the pre-chilled tubes.
   *The 4 ml total gives about 40 aliquots.*
9. Store immediately at −80 °C.

### F. Quality check

Transform a control plasmid of known quality (e.g. pUC19) and count colonies to check competence. If very few colonies appear, remake the solutions and cells.
